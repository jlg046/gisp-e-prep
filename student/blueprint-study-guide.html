Exam Blueprint & Study Guide

This page uses the new GISCI PreGISP Blueprint Companion materials as the roadmap for your preparation. The GISP-E is concept-based and software-agnostic; use the textbook, ESRI training, GIS Book of Knowledge, review sessions, and practice exams to build the understanding described here.

Know the exam

Question formatMultiple choice
Questions75
Time120 min
Blueprint domains11
Study principle: Do not memorize software menus. Focus on why concepts matter, when methods are appropriate, what assumptions and limitations matter, and how GIS ideas connect.

How the domains connect

1. Define the geographic problem2. Evaluate data3. Organize & document4. Select analysis5. Communicate results6. Consider systems, organizations, web & society

The 11 domains are not separate subjects to memorize. Treat them as connected parts of professional GIS reasoning.

GISCI readiness checklist

Before a progress check, ask whether you can do each of these without relying on software-specific terminology.

11 knowledge domains

Blueprint percentages are the authoritative measure of exam emphasis. Priority wording below is descriptive only.

18%

Analytical Methods & Geocomputation

Higher exam emphasis

Be comfortable with:

  • Spatial relationships and proximity
  • Buffer and overlay analysis
  • Raster and vector analytical models
  • Network analysis
  • Surface and terrain analysis
  • Interpolation
  • Suitability modeling
  • Spatial statistics at a conceptual level
  • Geoprocessing, automation, and reproducibility
Ask yourself: Why is a method appropriate, what assumptions does it make, and how should its results be interpreted?

Conceptual Foundations

Higher exam emphasis

Be comfortable with:

  • Spatial thinking and geographic representation
  • Coordinate reference systems and projections
  • Scale and resolution
  • Spatial relationships
  • Topology
  • Accuracy, precision, and uncertainty
  • Geographic abstraction and modeling

Data Modeling

Higher exam emphasis

Be comfortable with:

  • Conceptual, logical, and physical data models
  • Entities, attributes, feature classes, and tables
  • Relationships, keys, and normalization
  • Domains and attribute validation
  • Metadata and documentation
  • Data integrity and quality assurance
  • Enterprise databases and geodatabases conceptually
Ask yourself: How should information be organized so it can be stored, maintained, analyzed, and shared effectively?

Geospatial Data

Higher exam emphasis

Be comfortable with:

  • Vector and raster data
  • Primary and secondary data sources
  • Metadata and documentation
  • Accuracy, precision, and resolution
  • Completeness, consistency, and currency
  • Interoperability
  • Coordinate reference systems
  • Open, public, and proprietary data
  • Data stewardship and lifecycle

10%

Platforms, Sensors, Physical Principles & Image Processing

Moderate exam emphasis

Be comfortable with:

  • Remote sensing
  • Active and passive sensors
  • Satellite, aircraft, drone, and terrestrial platforms
  • GNSS and LiDAR fundamentals
  • Electromagnetic energy and spectral signatures
  • Spatial, spectral, temporal, and radiometric resolution
  • Image acquisition and calibration
  • Orthorectification, enhancement, classification, and feature extraction
  • DEMs and uncertainty
Ask yourself: What does a technology observe, what do its data represent, what are its limitations, and when is it appropriate?
