Workshop 6 Instructor Guide: GIS Organizations, Ethics, Society & Integrated Exam Review

Recommended length: 3 hours. Primary domains: Organizational & Institutional Aspects, GI & Society.

Review GIS project/program management, organizational implementation, professional practice, ethics, law/privacy, societal impacts, participation/equity, then integrate all 11 domains in exam-style decision making.

Instructional purpose

This session is designed as active retrieval and decision-making practice, not a software tutorial. Students should repeatedly have to identify the underlying GIS concept, justify a choice, reject plausible alternatives, and connect a gap to a specific BoK resource. Use software only when it makes an abstract distinction visible.

Preparation

  • Review the cohort administrator dashboard before class and identify the lowest-performing subdomains represented in this workshop.
  • Prepare 8 short warm-up prompts using language from the BoK index below; do not reuse exact practice-exam questions.
  • Have students work in pairs for the three hands-on activities, then require individual exit tickets.
  • Keep the official domain resource pages and the linked subtopic pages available for evidence checks when disagreements arise.

Facilitation plan and teaching notes

  1. 0–15 min — Retrieval warm-up: Eight one-minute prompts. Students answer individually, then compare. Record misconceptions without correcting immediately.
  2. 15–35 min — Concept map: Build a live map of the major distinctions in the domain(s). Ask students to supply examples and counterexamples.
  3. 35–70 min — Activity 1: Project-management scenario clinic: scope, requirements, staffing, risk, cost, and lifecycle decisions. Require a written justification and one rejected alternative.
  4. 70–80 min — Break.
  5. 80–120 min — Activity 2: Ethics & society case conference: privacy, access, participation, bias, representation, and professional obligations. Introduce at least one intentionally flawed case and have students diagnose it.
  6. 120–145 min — Activity 3: Integrated lightning round: 22 short scenarios—two from every blueprint domain. Have pairs exchange answers and challenge assumptions.
  7. 145–160 min — Exam-style challenge: Use new scenarios. After every item, ask “what fact makes the distractor wrong?”
  8. 160–180 min — Exit ticket: Collect one mastered distinction, one unresolved distinction, and three BoK links each student will review.

Answer / debrief framework

  1. Project choices should connect scope, stakeholders, requirements, resources, schedule, risk, governance, maintenance, and change control.
  2. Ethical analysis should identify affected parties, power/asymmetry, data limitations, privacy/access tensions, representation, participation, and professional obligations.
  3. For integrated review, score explanations as well as answers. A correct answer with a fragile rationale is not yet mastery.

Mastery evidence: A student should be able to choose correctly in a novel scenario and explain why at least one plausible alternative is wrong. Definitions alone are insufficient.

Adaptive teaching using tracker data

  • Cohort average below 70% in a relevant domain: spend 15–20 additional minutes on foundational distinctions and reduce activity breadth.
  • 70–79.9%: emphasize mixed scenarios and distractor analysis.
  • 80%+ but inconsistent students: use peer explanation and harder transfer cases rather than more lecture.
  • Repeated low performance for an individual: assign 3–5 precise BoK subtopics, then require a short teach-back before the next exam.
