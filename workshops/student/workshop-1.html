Workshop 1 Student Guide: Spatial Foundations, Reference Systems & Data Quality

Build a reliable mental model of geographic information, scale, coordinate/reference systems, data sources, metadata, accuracy, uncertainty, and fitness for use.

Conceptual FoundationsGeospatial Data

Learning goals

  • Explain the core terminology and conceptual distinctions represented in the assigned blueprint domains.
  • Select an appropriate GIS concept, data structure, method, or professional response when given a short scenario.
  • Identify common failure modes and explain why an alternative is more appropriate.
  • Connect missed practice-exam items to the exact GIS&T BoK page needed for remediation.

Before the session

  1. Open your Student Tracker and note your two lowest relevant domain scores.
  2. Review the domain resources below for 20–30 minutes, concentrating on unfamiliar headings rather than memorizing definitions.
  3. Bring one missed practice-exam item from a relevant domain and be ready to explain what confused you.
  • Conceptual Foundations (14%): https://geospacebok.eu/CF
  • Geospatial Data (11%): https://geospacebok.eu/GD

Hands-on agenda (3 hours)

  1. 0–15 min: Retrieval warm-up — 8 no-notes concept prompts.
  2. 15–35 min: Instructor concept map and misconception check.
  3. 35–70 min: Activity 1 — Projection triage: choose a defensible CRS for three mapping/analysis scenarios.
  4. 70–80 min: Break.
  5. 80–120 min: Activity 2 — Data-quality forensics: diagnose positional, attribute, temporal, logical-consistency, and completeness problems.
  6. 120–145 min: Activity 3 — Metadata scavenger hunt: decide whether a dataset is fit for a stated analysis.
  7. 145–160 min: Exam-style team challenge and explanation of distractors.
  8. 160–180 min: Individual exit ticket and remediation plan.

Student worksheet

  1. Define: Write five terms from the session in your own words. For each, add one “often confused with…” contrast.
  2. Choose: For each activity, record the method/concept you chose, two facts that justified it, and one tempting but incorrect alternative.
  3. Diagnose: Record one error source, assumption, limitation, or ethical issue that could invalidate the result.
  4. Transfer: Create one new scenario in which the same concept applies in a different application domain.
  5. Remediate: Select three BoK subtopics below for targeted review before your next practice exam.
Exit ticket: Without notes, explain one concept you can now distinguish confidently, one concept still uncertain, and exactly which resource you will use next.
